# 010 Simple AI Specification

## 1. Purpose

### Experimental resource-cycle policy

Native `original_live_start_wait_cost_source` effects may offer one own Active
Nijigasaki Stage Member as a cost, including the source. Costs are not modifier
targets. AI must consume the native candidate list and optional color flag;
Waiting Members are ineligible costs and their Blade does not fund Yell. This
source-object strategy is explicit; historical Stage-cost snapshots are not
silently upgraded. Default policy remains v1.1.

Cost-card choices may additionally require `requires_color_choice` and expose
`color_slots` in the same LegalAction. All policies must submit a legal cost and
color atomically or decline an optional ability; no engine payment occurs during
selection. Original-source departure is an explicit non-applied operation, not
an AI error-skip. Default policy remains unchanged.

All four policies consume native grouped bounds for
`public_named_groups_as_much_as_possible`: one candidate for each present name,
zero for an absent name. They must not bypass a required group, infer hidden
identities or reuse the old automatic first-match rule. This is a Rule Engine
accuracy fix; default policy and strategy version remain unchanged.

The offline blackbox runner may select a shared product policy explicitly and
load a frozen deck directory, seed base and one-based match start. Defaults keep
the historical heuristic. Each decision uses one controlling player's view;
unsupported mandatory effects stop in block mode or produce explicit error-skip
events in skip mode. Reports must not equate a not-activatable event or a skip
with resolved effects, and must count unfinished invocations separately.

`simple_ai_v1_2` is opt-in; new default matches remain `simple_ai_v1_1`.
It retains v1.1 protections and, only without a Live in hand, may set up to three
surplus Members offered by LegalActionGenerator, keeping the two highest-valued
Members. PDF 8.2.2/8.2.4 permits setting hand cards, not only Lives; normal draws,
face-down visibility and non-Live cleanup (10.5.1) remain Rule Engine operations.
No opponent private identity or future deck order may inform this choice.
AI-vs-AI acceptance counts initial and subsequent AI decisions against one cap;
Human-vs-AI retains its human-driver cap. Explained blockers alone do not prove
the separate completion-performance target. See the [verification report](../docs/development/ai-live-cycle-verification.md).

This spec defines how Simple AI should treat unsupported and manual card effects.

It does not define AI implementation code, algorithms, APIs, or UI.

## 2. Core Rule

Simple AI must choose from legal actions generated by the Rule Engine.

Simple AI must not bypass rule validation or directly mutate GameState.

Controller and LegalActionGenerator boundaries are owned by [012-controller-and-legal-actions.spec.md](012-controller-and-legal-actions.spec.md).

## 3. Effect Tag Usage

Simple AI may use `effect_tags` as heuristic hints.

Examples:

* prefer `draw` when hand size is low
* prefer `live_support` when attempting a Live
* prefer `energy_boost` when resource constrained
* deprioritize `manual_complexity` in AI vs AI debug mode

Tags do not make an effect executable.

## 4. Unsupported and Manual Effects

For `unsupported`, `tagged_only`, and `manual_resolution` effects, Simple AI must not assume automatic effect resolution.

Default AI vs AI debug policy for `manual_resolution` effects is `skip_and_log`.

Under `skip_and_log`, Simple AI ignores the unresolved semantic effect, records that the manual effect was skipped, and continues only through legal Actions.

Approximation by `effect_tags` is allowed only in explicit experimental mode. Silent auto-resolution is forbidden.

AI vs AI debug mode may support additional explicit run policies:

* `tag_approximation_experimental`: approximate by tags only when explicitly enabled
* `exclude_manual_cards`: exclude manual-resolution cards from the automated card pool

The chosen policy must be included in simulation output assumptions.

## 5. Determinism

Simple AI decisions must be deterministic when a seed is fixed.

Any random tie-break should be reproducible and logged.

Simple AI policy behavior is versioned independently from the runtime database schema.

* existing snapshots without an explicit policy version use `simple_ai_v0`
* snapshots created before the v1.1 policy keep their recorded `simple_ai_v1`
* newly created Simple AI matches use `simple_ai_v1_1`
* a replay must retain the policy version used when its decisions were produced

## 6. Rule Evaluation and Observation

Simple AI must consume a read-only rule evaluation produced by the Rule Engine rather than reimplementing effective Heart, Blade, Live score, or required-Heart calculations.

The AI observation boundary must include:

* the controller's own hand and public zones
* both players' public zones and success-Live progress
* only the count of the opponent's hidden hand, not card identities
* legal-action options intentionally exposed by the Rule Engine
* effective values required to compare legal Member, Live, and effect choices

Changing only the identities of cards in the opponent's hidden hand must not change a Simple AI decision.

## 7. Simple AI v1 and v1.1 Policies

`simple_ai_v1` enumerates legal candidates and assigns deterministic, explainable scores. It may score:

* opening-hand cost curve and Live Heart fit
* projected Stage Heart, Blade, and resource cost for Member placement
* effective Live requirements, expected Yell contribution, score, and match-point pressure
* structured effect benefit, resource cost, target value, color choice, count choice, and branch choice

The policy must not inspect candidates outside LegalActionGenerator output. A higher score is a controller preference, not permission to bypass validation.

Optional effects should be declined when their evaluated value is not positive. Unsupported mandatory effects follow the explicit manual-effect policy.

`simple_ai_v1_1` retains the same LegalAction-only boundary and adds conservative match-progress behavior:

* preserve a usable Live when it is the last practical winning resource in hand
* avoid replacing a Stage Member unless the evaluated gain is positive
* avoid setting redundant Live cards when one reachable Live can win and the opponent is not also at Match Point
* prefer draw and Live-recovery effects under Match Point pressure
* cap count-based Energy costs to the Active Energy candidates supplied by LegalActionGenerator

These preferences are strategy only. They must not alter legality, reveal hidden opponent cards, or reinterpret effect text.

## 8. Decision Logging and Performance

Each selected AI Action should record a compact decision summary containing:

* policy version
* selected Action type
* short reason
* selected score and a bounded component summary
* decision duration

Full candidate score lists belong in offline benchmark reports, not runtime events or snapshots.

The local benchmark target for maintained Simple AI policies is a p95 decision time no greater than 250 ms on the maintained acceptance environment.
